# Research map: scope and visual language

The map is an editorial index of the twelve publication records in `publications.js`, supplied in the author's CV. It adds no publication, authorship, experimental result or completed qualification.

Four overlapping questions organize the existing work: immunity and shared disease risk; microbes and metabolism; neurobiology and genetics; and platforms and resources. A paper may belong to more than one thread. The method groups are based on the recorded paper titles and summaries. They are a navigation aid, not an exhaustive account of each study's methods.

The microbiome–obesity and cytokine–COVID-19 papers connect to causal inference. The ATXN3 study connects to long-read sequencing/transcriptomics; the GLP-1R and cross-trait IBD studies connect to multi-omics. pGWAS-Portal and SV4GD connect to resources. AI exploration remains in the existing research scenes; it is not labelled as a published LLM result.

Connections indicate subject and method relationships. They do not depict proven biological causal effects or the chronological development of a research programme.

## Visual motifs

- A translucent, abstract cell motif: immune research, with membrane breathing and slowly moving internal accents.
- Drifting rods and circles: the diversity of microbial forms, not a species identification or a scale model.
- A branching neuron with travelling pulses: neurogenetics, used as a subject symbol rather than anatomical evidence.
- Floating data plates and nodes: research resources and computational platforms.
- Fine porcelain-inspired cloud ornament and curved ink paths: a visual connection to the site's existing art direction.

The four topic motifs are native SVG. The central data-ribbon sculpture and branch particles use a local Canvas 2D renderer with projected three-dimensional geometry; this abstract knot is not a molecular model. Both renderers use the five site palettes. Latin typography uses Times New Roman. Journal titles and the paper-specific gene symbols FCGR2A, Slc20a2 and ATXN3 remain italic; receptor/protein and pathway names are not indiscriminately italicized.

Desktop users can hover to preview, move into the explanation without losing the preview, click to pin, and navigate with the keyboard. Phone and tablet layouts rearrange the illustrated scene, reading area and connected-paper shelf. Selecting a paper retains its neighbours and reveals the explanation when it would otherwise be offscreen. The related-paper action visibly filters the publication list; its banner provides a return to all papers. Single-paper actions open the actual record and its details. Motion pauses offscreen, during the intro, in hidden tabs and while the celestial atlas is open; it respects global pause and reduced-motion preferences. No new external service is required.
